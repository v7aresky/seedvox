🌱 SeedVox

Speech that plans before it speaks

Smarter Speech AI — bigger data, smarter architecture, better prosody.

Most modern speech engines treat human expression like a brute-force problem: a single, bloated autoregressive model guesses emotion token by token. But emotion isn't a split-second dice roll — it's a global state of mind that shapes an entire sentence before we even open our mouths.

SeedVox splits the workload into three specialized components, so each one learns a clean job instead of juggling everything at once:

AR Phonetic Planner

The "what" — stable, hallucination-free phoneme planning and acoustic anchoring.

JEPA World Model

The "how" — reads the whole sentence at once and predicts a global prosody latent before generation begins.

AR Acoustic Decoder

The voice — turns text and the prosody plan into audio tokens, guided by the JEPA latent.

The results

The JEPA world model plans prosody before the decoder runs. The decoder then conditions on that plan and generates speech that actually follows it. The table below shows how well that works — measured across 200 evaluation batches (2,000 sentences).

How to read the scores

Audio-token loss (the first two rows) measures how accurately the decoder predicts each audio token. It's a cross-entropy loss — lower is better, and values below 1.5 are already quite good for a multilingual codec. The two numbers are compared against each other, not judged in isolation.

Cosine distance (the last two rows) measures how similar two prosody vectors are. It ranges from 0 (identical) to 1 (completely unrelated). Lower means the vectors are closer together.

MetricValueWhat it means
ar1.070Audio quality when the decoder sees the ground-truth prosody. This is the best-case ceiling — no system can do better than seeing the real answer.
gen_ar1.070Audio quality when the decoder sees a JEPA-sampled plan instead of ground truth. Same number as ar, which is the key result.
gen_cos0.237How close the sampled plan lands to the ground-truth prosody. 0.237 on a 0–1 scale means the plan is well-aligned with real prosody, while still allowing variation.
gen_cycle0.317How faithfully the decoder followed the plan it was given. 0.317 means the decoder doesn't perfectly reproduce the plan's exact shape — but it follows it closely enough that audio quality is unchanged.

The key result: gen_ar == ar (both 1.070). If the decoder were ignoring the prosody plan and just reading the text, gen_ar would be noticeably worse. It is exactly the same — the decoder genuinely follows the plan, and there is no text-only shortcut collapse. This proves the prosody latent is informative, not decorative.

A note on the reference voice

The reference speaker and training data is LJ Speech — a single-speaker audiobook corpus. LJ Speech is read speech: monotone, low expressiveness, no conversational dynamics. Expressive conversational reference and training data will sound considerably more natural and dynamic.
