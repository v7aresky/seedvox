SeedVox

Speech that plans before it speaks.

Smarter Speech AI — bigger data, smarter architecture, better prosody.

Today's speech engines treat expression like a brute-force problem: a single bloated autoregressive model guesses emotion token by token. But emotion is a global state of mind that shapes an entire sentence before we even speak. SeedVox separates the "what" from the "how" — and the "who" — with structural priors, so each component learns a clean job.

AR Phonetic Planner

the "what" Stable, hallucination-free phoneme planning & acoustic anchoring.

JEPA World Model

the "how" Reads the whole sentence at once, predicts a global prosody latent before generation.

Speaker Conditioning

the "who" A reference clip → speaker latent re-tunes phonemes & prosody to that voice via dual-FiLM — identity, not guessed.

AR Acoustic Decoder

the renderer Renders What + How + Who into audio tokens, guided by the JEPA latent.

Open-source research & education sandbox · Apache License 2.0 · github.com/v7aresky/seedvox

Motivation — against brute force

Big labs win by scale: enormous transformers, enormous data, brute-force token-by-token meaning. SeedVox argues that structure beats scale at the same budget.

Structure as leverage

Impose a principled prior: language sequencing (AR) vs. global emotional state (JEPA) separated explicitly — each component learns a clean job.

The frugality thesis: you don't need a giant lab's compute to get SOTA-quality voice. Split the job, bake in structure, and a single RTX 5090 trains and runs it — real-time for interaction, fully local, fully yours. Frugal, accessible, educational.

Data — enough, not massive

Structure beats scale extends to data: no proprietary corpora, no internet scrape. Five public speech sets — 276,529 utterances · 319.3 h — pre-cooked offline, so training just streams cached rows. Roughly 3 orders of magnitude smaller than a big lab's data budget (~1/1000th); <300 h unique — HiFiTTS & LibriTTS re-cut the same LibriVox audiobooks.

The JEPA World Model — the "how"

What is a JEPA? A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture predicts abstract latents, not raw signals — it is supervised by a similarity / energy objective (cosine), never by reconstruction.

Why a JEPA — vs. a pure AR LLM: an AR LLM must re-generate every token, so it guesses tone token-by-token and global intent drifts. A JEPA reads the whole sentence first (non-causal), spends no capacity on predictable acoustic detail, and returns a plan you can sample and edit.
  • Reads the whole text at once (non-causal) — predicts a global prosody latent spanning the utterance, before the decoder runs.
  • Naturally parallel — reads the whole text at once, no slow per-token roll.
  • Supervised by a frozen prosody codec trained from expressive speech (F0/E/V → latent), via a cosine/planning objective.
  • Learned stochasticity → sample plans for controlled expressivity.
  • Contrastive (0.2) + learned-std regularizers fight mean-collapse, keeping plans text-discriminative.
Result: a sampled plan is followed as faithfully as ground truth — the latent is informative, not decorative.

Latent, not features

F0/E/V feed only the frozen teacher. Generation conditions on the learned latent — (B, 32, dim), mean-pooled/upsampled to the frame rate.

jepa_layers=2 · jepa_heads=4 · plan (B, 32, dim) · teacher: frozen ProsodyCodec (F0/E/V → latent)

The "who" & the voice — speaker conditioning + acoustic decoder

Dual-FiLM disentanglement

FiLM = Feature-wise Linear Modulation — two independent FiLMAdapters turn the speaker latent into per-frame scale γ / shift β, then rescale-and-shift a feature:

Modulated = LayerNorm(x)·γ + β

  • film_phn — phoneme embeddings → articulation / vocal tract.
  • film_prs — prosody tokens → rhythm / intonation.

Why two? One flat speaker vector would bundle articulation with prosody → conditioning bleed: cloned voices get the wrong rhythm, and prosody edits disturb articulation. Independent adapters each learn a clean job → precise cloning, no bleed.

AR Acoustic Decoder

  • Streaming transformer over n_q=16 codebooks.
  • Cross-attention to fused text + speaker AdaLN per layer.
  • Depformer (NAR streaming transformer) predicts residual RVQ codebooks, with speaker + prosody AdaLN.
  • Mimi codec encodes waveform → token stream the decoder reconstructs.

dim=512 · layers=6 · depformer=10 · heads=8 · card=2048

Losses — every term has a job

A four-term fusion loss keeps responsibilities split: the "who" is conditioned per layer in the AR decoder, the "what" stays grounded, and the "how" is plan-driven.

LossEnforces
loss_ar ×1.0Audio quality: cross-entropy over each enabled RVQ codebook — curriculum 4 → 16 codebooks over 50k steps.
loss_ph ×0.5The "what": cross-entropy on the phoneme head (vocab 128) — pronunciation stays grounded even with fused text.
loss_jepa ×2.0The "how", three terms: ① 1−cos(plan, GT latent) — the plan must point like the true prosody; ② contrastive gap 0.2 — no mean-collapse, text-discriminative plans; ③ learned-std 0.05 — expressive variety at generation, no explosion.
loss_cycle ×0.5Plan-follow: cos(prosody of the generated audio, the plan) — the decoder is forced to use the latent, not read text.

Every responsibility has a targeted repo test — smoke train · Mimi fidelity · pitch sweep · g2p correctness · the plan-follow trio (gen_ar · plan_cos · follow_cos) scored every eval.

Results — the plan is followed, not ignored

Measured across 200 evaluation batches (2,000 sentences). One reference speaker, generated on a single RTX 5090.

How to read: audio-token loss — lower is better; cosine distance — 0 = identical → 1 = unrelated. plan_cos = sampled plan vs. true prosody; follow_cos = rendered audio vs. the plan.

MetricValueWhat it means
ar1.070Quality with ground-truth prosody — the best-case ceiling.
gen_ar1.070Quality with a JEPA-sampled plan — same as the ceiling.
plan_cos0.237Cosine distance — sampled plan vs. true prosody: how well the planner predicted the real prosody. Well-aligned, with room for variation.
follow_cos0.317Cosine distance — rendered audio's prosody vs. the plan: how faithfully the decoder followed it. Close enough that quality is unchanged.
The key result: gen_ar == ar (both 1.070). If the decoder ignored the plan and just read text, gen_ar would be worse. It isn't — no text-only shortcut collapse. The prosody latent is genuinely informative.

What finally worked

Three failure modes at first — text-only shortcut collapse, a prosody-lean AR head, planner mean-collapse — each broken by a targeted fix:

FixMechanism
GT / mix teacher-forcingCondition the decoder on GT prosody half the time (decoder_prosody_source='mix', decoder_gt_prob=0.5) so it learns latent→audio; sample a plan the rest to keep inference in-distribution.
Contrastive + stdThe regularizers from the JEPA intro — a matched-vs-mismatched contrastive gap (0.2) and a learned-std pull (0.05) — cure failure 3: measured text-discriminative, not average.
Direct depformer AdaLNInject mean-pooled prosody straight into the NAR codebook predictor (use_dep_prosody_cond) — bypass the prosody-lean AR hidden path.
Exagg dial (inference)Interpolate null ↔ sampled plan around the null latent — expressiveness control without retraining.
Cycle lossCompare the generated prosody against the plan it was conditioned on — the direct measurement that plans are followed.

Roadmap

Done: the hybrid AR-JEPA baseline — an autoregressive discrete-transformer decoder + JEPA + frozen prosody teacher, dual-FiLM conditioning, plan-follow verified (gen_ar == ar).

next · data Expressive conversational corpora

Longer-form context, richer prosody, real dialog structure — with natural-language emotion & style tags embedded in the text. Such datasets are emerging as TTS shifts toward expressive, multi-speaker dialogue.

Chunk-wise streaming inference and a full-duplex spoken channel — listen and speak simultaneously, for real-time conversation.
