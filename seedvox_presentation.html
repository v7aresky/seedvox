Technical deep-dive

🌱 SeedVox

Speech that plans before it speaks.

Smarter Speech AI — bigger, better data, smarter architecture, better prosody.

Today's speech engines treat expression like a brute-force problem: a single autoregressive model guesses emotion token by token. But emotion is a global state of mind that shapes an entire sentence before we even speak. SeedVox separates the "what" from the "how" — and the "who" — with structural priors, so each component learns a clean job.

AR Phonetic Planner

the "what" Stable, hallucination-free phoneme planning & acoustic anchoring.

JEPA World Model

the "how" Reads the whole sentence/context at once, predicts a global prosody latent before generation.

Speaker Conditioning

the "who" A reference clip → speaker latent re-tunes phonemes & prosody to that voice via FiLM adaptation — identity, not guessed.

AR Acoustic Decoder

the renderer Renders What + How + Who into audio tokens, guided by the JEPA latent.

Open-source research & education sandbox · Apache License 2.0 · github.com/v7aresky/seedvox

Motivation

Big labs win by scale: enormous transformers, enormous data, enormous finetuning efforts, brute-force token-by-token meaning. SeedVox argues that structure beats scale at the same budget.

Big labs → big compute and big data

Monolithic AR models hide expressiveness problems behind parameter and data counts most researchers and small labs can't afford.

Token-by-token emotion

Treating emotion as an autoregressive roll is brute force — it drifts and loses whole-sentence tension.

Hallucination & no low-level control

Frontier TTS hallucinates, stumbles on edge cases, and follows instructions inconsistently — and offers no fine control: you can't fix a mispronounced name or steer an pitch/prosody contour or timing.

That's exactly what SeedVox is designed to solve: an explicit AR phoneme planner (pronunciation control, robust g2p), an explicit JEPA prosody latent (steer prosody & timing), and structural priors that keep generation grounded — resisting hallucination and edge-case drift.
The frugality thesis: Do you need a giant lab's compute to get SOTA-quality voice? What if ... we split the job, bake in structure, and try a single RTX 5090 to train and run it — real-time for interaction, fully local and fully yours. Frugal, accessible, educational.

Offline pre-tokenization

Each utterance is resolved once into a .pt row — audio tokens (16×RVQ) · text · phoneme ids · acoustic features (F0/E/V) — no live feature extraction in training.

Frame-aligned prosody

log-F0 (median-filtered) and log-energy at 12.5 Hz — aligned to the audio tokens.

Center, don't standardize

Subtract per-utterance median log-F0 / mean log-energy, but keep the variance — excursions stay meaningful.

Roughly 3 orders of magnitude smaller — about 1/1000th of a big lab's data budget but audiobook reading style biased — yet curated could beat volume: the prosody branch learns from real F0/energy/voicing variation, and amortizing preprocessing once keeps training on a single RTX 5090 routine. Under 300 h unique: HiFiTTS and LibriTTS re-cut the same LibriVox audiobooks — counting shared recordings once, the unique corpus is <300 h — the frugality claim was already conservative.
Typical TTS training data includes: normalized text (numbers, abbreviations & punctuation expanded to how people say them) paired with the speech waveform, a speaker labels, optionally dialogue context (who said it, to whom, in what exchange), and increasingly natural-language labels describing the speech and its emotion — a "lively", "excited" or "annoyed" caption. SeedVox currently uses none of the labels: expression is supervised directly from the audio — the prosody codec converts audio features F0/E/V to a latent, so the "how" is measured, not captioned.

Key design choices

JEPA prosody planning

Prosody is a global state of mind, not a token-level dice roll. The planner emits a learned latent that shapes the whole utterance before generation.

Latent, not features

Raw F0 / Energy / Voicing acoustic features only feed the frozen codec as a training target. Generation conditions exclusively on the learned latent.

Unified linguistic fusion

A gated cross-attention layer folds the AR phoneme planning into the text backbone — one grounded, unified representation for acoustic generation.

Dual-FiLM disentanglement

Two speaker-modulated FiLM adapters separate articulation from rhythm / emotion.

Intervenable control

Phonemes are planned explicitly — overwrite the phoneme string at runtime to fix pronunciations.

The painter — AR/NAR renderer

The plans become audio: a streaming AR transformer hands off to a parallel NAR depformer, rendering unified text + prosody plan into discrete audio tokens.

Efficient by construction

Gradient checkpointing, Fused AdamW, torch.compile — inference under 400 ms on an RTX 5090.

  • Char + BPE encoding — character embeddings enriched by a gated BPE subword encoder (word-level / morphology context).
  • AR phoneme head (vocab 128) — explicit, intervenable pronunciation anchor; a regularizer on the fused text.
  • Alignment is attention-based: the decoder cross-attends to the unified text via a monotonic cross-attention layer — no duration model drives timing.
  • Unified Linguistic Fusion — solving "tri-alignment": one fused representation instead of parallel Audio↔Text and Audio↔Phoneme alignments.

The JEPA World Model — the "how"

What is a JEPA? A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture predicts abstract latents, not raw signals — it is supervised by a similarity / energy objective (cosine), never by reconstruction.

Why a JEPA — vs. a pure AR LLM: an AR LLM must re-generate every token, so it guesses tone token-by-token and global intent drifts. A JEPA reads the whole sentence first (non-causal), spends no capacity on predictable acoustic detail, and returns a plan you can sample and edit.
  • Reads the whole text at once (non-causal) — predicts a global prosody latent spanning the utterance, before the decoder runs.
  • Naturally parallel — reads the whole text at once, no slow per-token roll.
  • Supervised by a frozen prosody codec trained from expressive speech (F0/E/V → latent), via a cosine/planning objective.
  • Learned stochasticity → sample plans for controlled expressivity.
  • Contrastive (0.2) + learned-std regularizers fight mean-collapse, keeping plans text-discriminative.
Result: a sampled plan is followed as faithfully as ground truth — the latent is informative, not decorative.

Latent, not features

F0/E/V feed only the frozen teacher. Generation conditions on the learned latent — (B, 32, dim), mean-pooled/upsampled to the frame rate.

jepa_layers=2 · jepa_heads=4 · plan (B, 32, dim) · teacher: frozen ProsodyCodec (F0/E/V → latent)

Layout in context

Decoder context = [speaker, adapted prosody, fused text].

The "who" & the voice — speaker conditioning + acoustic decoder

Dual-FiLM disentanglement

FiLM = Feature-wise Linear Modulation — two independent FiLMAdapters turn the speaker latent into per-frame scale γ / shift β, then rescale-and-shift a feature:

Modulated = LayerNorm(x)·γ + β

  • film_phn — phoneme embeddings → articulation / vocal tract.
  • film_prs — prosody tokens → rhythm / intonation.

Why two? One flat speaker vector would bundle articulation with prosody → conditioning bleed: cloned voices get the wrong rhythm, and prosody edits disturb articulation. Independent adapters each learn a clean job → precise cloning, no bleed.

AR Acoustic Decoder

  • Streaming transformer over n_q=16 codebooks.
  • Cross-attention to fused text + speaker AdaLN per layer.
  • Depformer (NAR streaming transformer) predicts residual RVQ codebooks, with speaker + prosody AdaLN.
  • Mimi codec encodes waveform → token stream the decoder reconstructs.

dim=512 · layers=6 · depformer=10 · heads=8 · card=2048

Losses & tests — every term has a job

A four-term fusion loss keeps responsibilities split: the "who" is conditioned per layer in the AR decoder, the "what" stays grounded, and the "how" is plan-driven. Each guide is verified by a targeted test.

LossEnforces
loss_ar ×1.0Audio quality: cross-entropy over each enabled RVQ codebook — curriculum 4 → 16 codebooks over 50k steps.
loss_ph ×0.5The "what": cross-entropy on the phoneme head (vocab 128) — pronunciation stays grounded even with fused text.
loss_jepa ×2.0The "how", three terms:
① 1−cos(plan, GT latent) — the plan must point like the true prosody (cosine sees shape, not volume);
② contrastive gap 0.2 — the plan must win over every other text's latent in the batch by a margin ⇒ no mean-collapse, text-discriminative plans;
③ learned-std 0.05 — a light pull keeping the sampler's learned scale near its init ⇒ expressive variety at generation, no explosion.
loss_cycle ×0.5Plan-follow: cos(prosody of the generated audio, the plan) — the decoder is forced to use the latent, not read text. Anchor term 0.5 keeps the readout honest.

Duration auxiliary (0.08) is defined but not used for alignment — alignment is attention-based (MonotonicAttention).

Results — the plan is followed, not ignored

Measured across 200 evaluation batches (2,000 sentences). One reference speaker, generated on a single RTX 5090.

How to read the scores

  • Audio-token loss (first two rows): cross-entropy on predicted tokens — lower is better, values below 1.5 are already quite good for a multilingual codec. Compare the pair.
  • Cosine distance (last two rows): similarity of two prosody vectors, 0 = identical → 1 = unrelated. Lower is closer. plan_cos compares the sampled plan to the true prosody; follow_cos compares the rendered audio's prosody back to the plan.
MetricValueWhat it means
ar1.070Quality with ground-truth prosody — the best-case ceiling.
gen_ar1.070Quality with a JEPA-sampled plan — same as the ceiling.
plan_cos0.237Cosine distance — sampled plan vs. true prosody: how well the planner predicted the real prosody. Well-aligned, with room for variation.

Lessons learned — plugging JEPA latents into an AR decoder

Feeding a global, non-causal prosody latent into a sequential, causal token decoder is the hardest integration in SeedVox. Three failure modes, then what fixed each.

1 · Text-only shortcut collapse

Conditioning on the predicted plan (a deterministic function of text) makes the latent redundant — the decoder quietly ignores it.

decoder_prosody_source='pred' ⇒ ignored latent

2 · The AR head barely absorbs it

A probe of the AR decoder's hidden state showed it barely carries prosody — passing the latent only via AR context leaks nothing downstream.

probe ⇒ hidden state ≈ prosody-lean

3 · Planner mean-collapse

Without extra pressure, predicted plans hug the average direction — informative (global) but not discriminative across texts.

plans → mean · "decorative" latent

What finally worked

FixMechanism
GT / mix teacher-forcingCondition the decoder on GT prosody half the time (decoder_prosody_source='mix', decoder_gt_prob=0.5) so it learns latent→audio; sample a plan the rest to keep inference in-distribution.
Contrastive + stdThe regularizers from the JEPA intro — a matched-vs-mismatched contrastive gap (0.2) and a learned-std pull (0.05) — cure failure 3: measured text-discriminative, not average.
Direct depformer AdaLNInject mean-pooled prosody straight into the NAR codebook predictor (use_dep_prosody_cond) — bypass the prosody-lean AR hidden path.
Exagg dial (inference)Interpolate null ↔ sampled plan around the null latent — expressiveness control without retraining.
Cycle lossCompare the generated prosody against the plan it was conditioned on — the direct measurement that plans are followed.

Roadmap

done Hybrid AR-JEPA baseline

Autoregressive discrete-transformer decoder + JEPA World Model + frozen prosody teacher; Unified Linguistic Fusion; dual-FiLM speaker conditioning; plan-follow verified (gen_ar == ar).

next · data Expressive conversational corpora

Longer-form context, richer prosody, real dialog structure — paired with natural-language emotion and style tags embedded in the text to seed the style head and let the planner learn from richer supervision signals. We expect such datasets to become available as TTS research shifts toward expressive, multi-speaker dialogue.

beyond · explored Continuous-latent acoustic decoder

Graft a flow-matching continuous-latent acoustic decoder over Mimi latents so the prosody signal lands without a codebook-quantization bottleneck.

beyond · unexplored Mid- & post-training finetuning

DPO / GRPO-style preference finetuning — mid-training or post-training on human/AI preferences — has not been explored yet: we've worked with a very limited data budget. LoRA adaptation is already supported for fast, lightweight voice adaptation.

beyond Streamability & full-duplex channel

Chunk-wise streaming inference and a full-duplex spoken channel — listen and speak simultaneously, enabling real-time conversational interaction.

Takeaways

  • SeedVox is smarter speech AI: structural priors instead of brute-force scale — separate the "what" (AR phonemes) from the "how" (JEPA prosody).
  • Unified linguistic fusion: characters + BPE subwords + explicit AR phoneme planning converge into one text representation the model plans from.
  • The Thinker & Painter split resolves the stability↔expressiveness trade-off with a hybrid discrete-AR + JEPA design.
  • Measured: the decoder follows the plan (gen_ar == ar); no text-shortcut collapse.
  • Fully open-sourced — training, inference, and model configurations all included; 284M parameters including the speech renderer, <400 ms inference on an RTX 5090, Apache 2.0.
  • Learns to speak fast — after 12 epochs on the single-speaker LJSpeech corpus, in under one hour of training on a single GPU, the model already produces intelligible speech that follows the input text — a fast iteration cycle for researchers learning speech science.
  • It's getting attention — +25k views on LinkedIn and PhD students reaching out to collaborate.
